# Working Papers & Research Essays

This repository collects developing articles that examine specific cases and problems involving knowledge, authority, responsibility, and learning.

Each article develops a standalone argument through close analysis of its subject. The collection offers an entry into a broader research programme, with links to the underlying frameworks provided within individual articles.

## Articles

### [When an Average Becomes a Fact](articles/when-an-average-becomes-a-fact.md)

*The Changing Authority of a Mathematical Object*

How does a calculated average become evidence about someone’s actual circumstances—and acquire the authority to support a consequential decision?

Using a hypothetical income-assessment example, this article follows a mathematical result through calculation, interpretation, administrative acceptance, and challenge. It examines how the number can remain unchanged while the claims and authority attached to it expand.

**Status:** Working draft
**Related framework:** Four Epistemic Orientations (FEO)

### [Who Decided Before You Pulled the Lever?](articles/who-decided-before-you-pulled-the-lever.md)

*Authority and Responsibility in the Trolley Problem*

What has the trolley problem already decided before it asks the respondent to choose?

This article examines how the question distributes agency, control, and responsibility. It considers what the constructed choice can reveal, and what further justification is needed when its conclusions are extended into transport design and governance.

**Status:** Working draft
**Related method:** Protocol of Right-Leveling (PRL)

## About the Drafts

These articles are shared for reading, discussion, and revision. They are not peer-reviewed publications. Arguments, terminology, examples, and conclusions may change as the work develops.

Hypothetical examples are identified within the text. References, version information, and article-specific limitations are maintained in the individual drafts.

## Feedback

Feedback is welcome, particularly on:

* Inferences the analysis does not adequately support.
* Relevant features of a case that have been overlooked.
* Alternative explanations or counterexamples.
* Existing research that would sharpen or challenge the argument.

When commenting, please identify the article and passage concerned.
